# M6 Evidence v2 checkpoint

The existing content-addressed observation envelope reads both versions. A legacy v1 record can be upgraded explicitly to v2 in memory. Persisting a v1 detection omits the v2 field, preserving its former content hash. Both versions retain photo/candidate checks and detector fallback when the optional provider fails.

The visibility consumer is explicitly allowlisted. `regional.landmark_visibility_cap@v1` describes a photo-local upper bound on landmark visibility for a named region. A record must specify its uncertainty. The consumer compares uncertainty-expanded ranges, ignores contradictory bounds and uses the least restrictive upper bound among compatible observations. Duplicate input supplies no extra confidence. It can only reduce the native visibility used by alignment and fitting. Existing v1 hidden-region suppression keeps priority.

Circumference, asymmetry, morphological descriptors and volume proxies can be validated and stored for later evaluation. Their definitions need known-effect consumers and conflict checks before driving geometry. Metric inputs require a calibration reference and positive uncertainty. Metric inputs remain outside the shape allowlist.

The shape allowlist also supports `arm.forearm_to_upperarm@v1`. It is the side-specific distance from the elbow joint centre to the wrist joint centre divided by the distance from the shoulder joint centre to the elbow joint centre, in subject-local coordinates. The fit compares this ratio to the corresponding canonical rig joint centres. The consumer requires an aligned photo, an observed arm region, visibility of at least 0.8 and positive uncertainty. It accepts ratios from 0.25 to 2.0 and uncertainty up to 0.5. Compatible intervals form a conservative containing envelope, and contradictory measurements for a side disable that side's contribution. The penalty per side is capped at 0.02 in the internal fit objective. It leaves reported silhouette scores, the existing silhouette fallback and the legacy/default path intact. The known-effect regression exercises both the measured geometry and candidate selection. Other shape definitions remain diagnostic until qualified.
