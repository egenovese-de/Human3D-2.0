# M6 rig and geometry transfer checkpoint

The Lab uses right-handed column-vector algebra and converts Blender XYZ into canonical XYZ as (X, Z, −Y). Six algebraic controls verified both rotation signs about all three mapped axes. Two native projects with 163 bones each closed the rest chain within 4.45e-16 native units and the posed chain within 3.56e-15 maximum matrix-entry difference. The native posed-global times inverse-bind identity matched the skin pose transforms within 1.78e-15. Twelve source fixtures closed their recorded local basis on the homologous direction chains within 1.13e-7 maximum matrix-entry difference. See the [pinned audit](https://github.com/egenovese-de/Human3D-2.0/blob/28d16bfad33da7b203b94e0306ab4fe42d860b61/DOCS/m6/rig-bind-audit.json).

M6.2-005 requires independently confirmed detector-equivalent semantics for the full landmark set. The Q joint-centre projections and facial picks cover a subset, with single-review surface picks and no independently confirmed mapping for eyes, ears, digits, heels and toes. These remain proxy observations. M6.2-007 requires verified homologous transverse bind axes, particularly for wrists, feet and pelvis/spine. The canonical USD bind transforms have global identity orientation and the native segment data leave axial twist underdetermined. Source and native matrix closure cannot resolve that correspondence. The audit records the cross-rig angular error as unavailable. The release pose estimator remains the comparison baseline.

The candidate transferred four Q identities through the canonical geometry compatibility probe. Both 12 mm and 24 mm variants failed face-area validation on all four identities. The exact canonical control, 3 mm and 0.6 mm damped controls, and native reconstructed comparator passed native rebind and pose in all four identities and preserved topology, UVs and skin. The accepted damped controls bound the tested compatibility behavior for these fixtures. Cross-topology replacement remains gated by independently demonstrated geometric and structural gains.
