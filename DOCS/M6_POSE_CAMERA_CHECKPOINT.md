# M6 fixed-geometry pose and camera checkpoint

The 32-condition Q replay evaluated four articulated identities, two estimation cameras, native versus independent segment-direction inputs, and bootstrap versus fitted shape. The evaluation camera and registration were fixed. With bootstrap geometry and the native fitting camera, substituting the direction proxy changed mean held-out silhouette IoU from 61.84% to 68.84%. The 16-condition calibrated-camera raster-mask control changed fitted held-out IoU from 60.24% to 66.15% and symmetric posed surface error from 34.24 mm to 26.11 mm. These are sensitivities under synthetic controls. The camera comparison measures compensation between camera assumptions and pose input. It supplies no calibrated camera confidence threshold.

See the [fixed-geometry baseline](https://github.com/egenovese-de/Human3D-2.0/blob/d2fab9733c2d14e7bb5227ca1a7e558c50b85a89/DOCS/m6/fixed-geometry-pose-camera.json).

A depth-attenuation variant scaled inferred limb depth by the weaker landmark confidence above the existing threshold and kept image-plane directions fixed. Across eight conditions from four identities, it reduced mean direction error from 18.59° to 18.25° and mean head error from 86.57 mm to 85.76 mm. Held-out IoU decreased from 61.85% to 61.30%, with a loss in all eight conditions. It missed the 1° angular gain and no-IoU-regression promotion gates. Retain the native estimator. The reported sample has four identity units because the poses share identities. See the [screen](https://github.com/egenovese-de/Human3D-2.0/blob/867c8b888375c6f8080cca77adffdb012bf91de9/DOCS/m6/limb-confidence-screen.json).

The depth and full rotation work remains gated by independent landmark semantics and the cross-rig bind-frame correspondence. Axial twist, wrist/foot and contact changes require the same verified input. The geometry, appearance and export investigations can use the fixed release pose while those dependencies are unresolved.

The pose-first, camera-first and alternating Lab schedules each used 17 evaluations of the same training view and frozen geometry. Each tested the left forearm direction plus camera translation and scale with the same search bounds and steps. All three increased mean held-out IoU from 61.84% to 62.26%. Mean independent forearm direction error increased from 7.03° to 9.80°. The result exposes pose and camera compensation and rejects promotion of these orders under the direction constraint. See the [schedule replay](https://github.com/egenovese-de/Human3D-2.0/blob/cbc0802bca35d0cd1927c1984402492726ca14ac/DOCS/m6/pose-camera-schedule-screen.json).
